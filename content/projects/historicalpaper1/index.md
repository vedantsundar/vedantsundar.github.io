“Do Not Burrow in Other People’s Yards!”

Berg. *Nicht in fremden Gӓrten wühlen!* Illustration. April 25, 1956. Neues Deutschland. (Full citation on pg. 17, Appendix B, of full paper.)

This paper concerns Operation Gold, a Cold War-era American wiretapping operation that required a 1,476-foot tunnel to be constructed under Berlin, and its significance in history.

While looking through books at my local library, I happened to come across the book *Betrayal in Berlin* by Steve Vogel. I became fascinated by the thrilling topic, partly due to its bold nature, and started to research more about the operation. Eventually, I was able to find declassified Central Intelligence Agency documents, which made the connection of this topic to the theme of Frontiers in History very clear.

As someone who enjoys critical analysis, I chose to present my research in the historical paper category. There were not a lot of clear photographs taken due to the discrete nature of the operation, which influenced me to select the paper category; I also felt that the topic would best match with a paper.

I conducted initial research using multiple sources from Betrayal in Berlin, and soon found the Central Intelligence Agency’s official documents on Operation Gold, housed on the Internet Archive. With a multitude of sources available to cross-check the construction phase, I started to draft the American and British engineers’ processes for building the tunnel. My main focus quickly shifted to showing the reader how risky yet crucial the operation was, to showcase the true extent of the operation.

Once I had an understanding of the tunnel’s construction, I structured my research into three main parts. I researched and wrote a background on the tensions leading up to the operation by consulting primary sources of those involved in the project’s inception and general background on the Cold War’s start. Next, I analyzed the Soviet Union’s reception of Operation Gold, mainly by finding Berlin newspaper commentary and caricatures. The Central Intelligence Agency had created a report devoted to the press reception of the operation, which was very helpful in obtaining newspapers that were not present in archival collections. Lastly, the significance of the tunnel for the United States was written, using the reported outcome of the operation from the Central Intelligence Agency, as well as American newspapers.

Operation Gold proved to be invaluable as a frontier in history. The Central Intelligence Agency, previously thought to be nowhere near the prowess of the Komitet Gosudarstvennoy Bezopasnosti (KGB), formed a name for itself. This led to the United States gaining power over the whole world as even KGB officials were astonished at the scale of the operation. Operation Gold inspired the United States to pursue bolder espionage operations, directly promoting the United States’ growth to become a global superpower. The Central Intelligence Agency was also forced to partner with their ‘enemy’ — the National Security Agency — directly leading to the United States maintaining an intelligence grasp on the whole world. 

Thus, the following paper makes the argument that Operation Gold advanced the United States’ intelligence powers, and became a frontier in counterintelligence and espionage.

A full paper is available upon request, and was submitted to the Texas History Day competition in 2023.

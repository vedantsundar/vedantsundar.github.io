I began researching general topic areas for the Texas History Day contest and came upon India’s independence. While the area was completely unfamiliar, it somehow appealed to my interests. However, this intriguing topic was a bit too broad. After a discussion with my family, I was certain that my topic would be regarding India’s independence. I also learned about Dr. B.R. Ambedkar’s fight for the ‘Untouchable’ caste, or the Dalits. While reading about Ambedkar, the topic of the Round Table Conferences came up, and after doing rudimentary research, I decided that the Round Table Conferences were perfect. Coincidentally, this topic also aligned with the annual theme: the debates between India and Great Britain were filled with diplomacy, and demonstrated many successes and failures. In addition, there were a plethora of consequences resulting from the Round Table Conferences.

At the start of my research, I was able to obtain basic knowledge via secondary sources. When looking for more secondary sources, I stumbled on the University of Nottingham’s website. On their webpage, the official Round Table Conferences’ documents were declassified, which allowed me to view the proceedings of the conferences written by the Secretary of State of India (Mr. Wedgewood Benn, and then Sir Samuel Hoare) at the time. Reading through the official documents gave me a thorough understanding of both sides, and afterward, I looked for extra secondary sources to corroborate information and to eliminate any possible bias in the primary sources.

While researching, I kept a tabular document containing my analysis and the information collected from various sources. This aided me in creating my historical argument. I chose to write a historical paper as there were not many photographs taken during the conference, and the primary source documents were all printed works, with no pictures included. Additionally, writing about history gives me so much pleasure that it felt justified to select this category at first glance. Almost immediately, I began to draft sections of my historical paper. Once my ideas were written out, I began to revise and order paragraphs to form my paper. 

The Round Table Conferences are significant in numerous ways. They were a direct influence for the Government of India Act of 1935, which acted as a precedent to the Purna Swaraj Resolution’s royal assent in 1947. Unfortunately, between 1935 and 1947, the Round Table Conferences were nullified due to World War Ⅱ. The Round Table Conferences also showed the world that India was a diplomatic power. Additionally, it set the stage for the state of India’s government and helped form two of the world’s largest democracies, India and the United Kingdom. Lastly, by utilizing debate and diplomacy, the Round Table Conferences forged a link with Great Britain. Hence, I argue that the Round Table Conferences were vital in India’s independence and created a secure alliance with Great Britain, but were overshadowed due to various tensions at the time.

abstract: Anderson Localization is a phenomenon negating wave diffusion in a spatially-disordered medium and currently is only a theoretical concept in Phononics. To attain true spatial distortion, the Phononic Crystal medium should not have a characteristic Bravais Lattice, which may be satisfied through a Fluid-Fluid Ferrofluid medium in a magnetic field. Further enhancement of Anderson Localization effects may result from a topological defect allowing for Soliton propagation. Thus, the purpose of this project is to develop a Ferrofluidic waveguide system with a topological defect under a controlled Magnetic Field to physically observe Anderson Localization. The success of this project will be determined via simulated degrees of Acoustic Pressure damping (using COMSOL® Multiphysics 5.6). The experimental setup was created through a CAD kernel, simulating Magnetic Flux Density and Total Acoustic Pressure. Nine periods of N52-model magnets were placed along edges of a 400-millimeter air-environment block to form a periodic structure (control); a vertical deviation of $\frac{25}{6}$ millimeters in the 5th and 6th magnet periods formed the topological defect. Compared to the sinusoidal Total Acoustic Pressure plot of the control, the defect-induced Total Acoustic Pressure plot experienced trigger peak damping, demonstrating the ultrasound-isolation effect of Anderson Localization. The use of commercially-available components further offers practicality. Therefore, the Ferrofluidic waveguide system developed successfully demonstrates the effects of Anderson Localization in Phononics and can be physically realized. The setup realized may be applied to future Phononic localization effects and can aid in the practical optimization of speaker systems using Ferrofluids.
